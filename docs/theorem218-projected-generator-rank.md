# Theorem 2.18: projected generating supports and the boundary cost

Checkpoint: 9 October 2026, PR #172. This supplements the positive proof
plan from PRs #170--171. The working closed-test completion convention
remains explicitly named; no literal 2019 predicate or manuscript prose
is changed. Consult the PR's final build and permitted-axiom audit before
marking the new declarations validated.

## 1. Closure transport requires only a positive relational map

For any positive relational map p:E->D,

    p[cl_U^E(S)] is contained in cl_U^D(p[S]).

Proof: inverse images of relative U-substructures under p are relative
U-substructures. Apply minimality of the source hull. There is no source
or target closedness requirement in this elementary transport assertion.

If S generates E, the entire range p[E] lies in cl_U^D(p[S]). The hull of
p[E] equals the hull of p[S], but neither raw image is declared closed.
A working closed-test map therefore factors through that induced target
hull, with the same vertex map. The weak image remains a distinct object.

Implementation: `Ramsey/ClosureProjectedGenerators.lean`.

## 2. The collision branch is a genuine completion theorem

Assume D is finite and U-closed, and all its closed substructures of U-size
at most j have K-completions. Suppose E is generated by a finite G with
|G| <= j+1 and has a working closed-test map p into D. If x,y are distinct
members of G with p(x)=p(y), then

    |p[G]| < |G| <= j+1,
    USize(D induced on cl_U^D(p[G])) <= |p[G]| <= j.

Complete that projected hull and precompose with the factored map from E.
`HasClosedUKCompletion.of_generator_collision` concludes a K-completion
of E; it is not merely a cardinality estimate. The proof deletes one of
the redundant generators in the exact finite image calculation.

The more general `of_projected_generators` uses the projected support
budget directly. No |hull| bound or equality of weak and closed ranks
occurs. No minimality of G is required.

## 3. Common boundary data now follow from one ambient projection

`ClosureProjectedRankGlue.lean` constructs the previous theorem's Q,
its two embeddings, the projected side sources, and all commuting maps.

Inputs are a free source amalgam E amalgam_R F, a U-closed R, semi-closed
sides, one working projection p to a finite U-closed D, and a closed A-copy
in D containing the raw projected separator. A belongs to K; K is closed
under closed substructures and consists of ordinary irreducibles.

For finite target supports JL,JR with |JL|,|JR| <= j, suppose

    p[E] is contained in cl_U^D(JL),
    p[F] is contained in cl_U^D(JR).

Take Q = D induced on cl_U^D(p[R]). It lies in the selected closed A-copy,
so is in K and irreducible. Minimality puts that SAME Q into each side
hull. Each side hull has U-size <= j and is completed by the rank invariant.
The previous independent-completion gluing lemma then completes the whole
source. The theorem does not assume side completions, a chosen common
target, or a simultaneous-extension diagram.

`of_common_projection_generators` derives the displayed range inclusions
from generating sets of the two sides. The numeric projected budgets
remain explicit premises.

## 4. Actual free-amalgam generator geometry

`ClosureFreeAmalgamGenerators.lean` proves, for a generating set G of the
WHOLE free source, that

    iL^{-1}[G] union range(sL) generates E,
    iR^{-1}[G] union range(sR) generates F.

These are statements about relative closure from the actual tuple
free-amalgamation law. They do not assume source-root closedness or finite
cardinality. To prove the first, extend any closed subset of E containing
its indicated generators by the entire opposite side; this is a relative
U-substructure of the whole, so it contains G and hence all vertices.

The resulting projection theorem shows that cl_U^D(JL) contains p[E]
provided it contains p[R] and the projections of G lying on E. The right
version is symmetric. This proves the whole-side range inclusions from
boundary-and-generator data, rather than assuming them as an oracle.

`HasClosedUKCompletion.of_whole_generated_projection_supports` combines
that geometry with the previous mixed rank theorem. It uses only one
generating set G of the whole source, not separate certificates that
intersecting G with the two sides generates them.

## 5. The remaining cut/support lemma (NOT proved by this patch)

For the injective-generator case in the actual Picture refinement, construct
a cut and finite target supports JL,JR of size at most j such that each hull
contains the projected boundary AND the projected generators on its side.
Do not omit the boundary cost. Losing a generator on the opposite side
does not itself prove that the chosen side is generated by the remaining
ones. The exact generator law in Section 4 records the missing contribution.

Containment of Q in A ensures membership in K and permits target gluing;
it does NOT itself bound the number of generators needed to generate Q.
The boundary must be generated within both small target supports. Nor is
the collision corollary available for a projection that does not preserve
the protected tests required by the working convention.

The next construction task is therefore to extract these two supports from
the selected Picture/Hales--Jewett history and prove their budgets. If the
history does not supply them, a candidate alternative is a rank invariant
relative to a fixed closed K-boundary; that is a proposed strengthening
requiring its OWN base, step and local-finiteness proof, not a present input
or a substitute for the missing cut lemma.
