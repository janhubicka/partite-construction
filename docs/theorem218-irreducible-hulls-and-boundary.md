# Theorem 2.18: irreducible hulls and the relative boundary reduction

9 October 2026, PR #173. Positive continuation of #170--172.
The literal 2019 predicates and frozen manuscript remain unchanged.
Primary source for the surrounding proof: Hubicka--Nesetril,
*All those Ramsey classes*, arXiv:1606.07979v4, Sections 2.1.2 and 2.5.

## 1. Closed hulls of ordinary irreducible seeds are protected

A U-closed structure generated by an embedded ordinary irreducible seed
is intrinsically U-irreducible. Indeed, in any free decomposition into
closed sides the seed lies on one side; that side is a relative
U-substructure of the whole and therefore contains its entire closure.
The other side cannot make the decomposition proper on both sides.

Consequences proved in `Ramsey/ClosureIrreducibleHulls.lean`:

- The ambient U-closure of any ordinary irreducible induced seed in a
  U-closed structure is a protected closed U-irreducible test.
- A working closed-test map from a U-closed source embeds this WHOLE hull.
  The original seed need not be closed.
- Such a map is an ordinary relational homomorphism-embedding.
- If both source and target are closed, then for an ordinary irreducible
  seed S, f[cl_U(S)] = cl_U(f[S]), and f is injective on cl_U(S).

The last equality is stronger than #172's general inclusion, but only
because this particular hull is protected. It is NOT asserted for an
arbitrary reducible generating set, nor does it make the raw set f[S]
closed. It also does not identify the working closed-test map predicate
with the literal intrinsic 2019 U-homomorphism-embedding predicate.

The proof includes singleton seeds. Empty seeds cause no exception in
the general generating-seed statement.

## 2. A genuine rank-one base and stronger local-test extraction

`Ramsey/ClosureRankOneBase.lean` proves that every U-closed structure
of U-size at most one is U-irreducible. A finite generating set attaining
the minimum has at most one vertex, so its induced seed is ordinarily
irreducible. This includes rank zero, without an artificial nonemptiness
hypothesis.

Hence, once an actual picture has protected-test coverage by a fixed
finite irreducible B in K, every closed rank-at-most-one substructure
has a full embedding into B and therefore a working K-completion. This
proves the completion part of the first positive rank invariant under
that coverage hypothesis; it does not construct the Ramsey picture.

The local-test extraction can now be strengthened as well. To complete
an arbitrary weak induced S with |S|<=n, complete the CLOSED hull of S
using the rank-n invariant. The map on that closed hull is an ORDINARY
homomorphism-embedding by Section 1. Precompose the exact inclusion of S.
The same finite irreducible K-target is an ordinary completion of S.
No new closure hull is substituted for the tested S in the conclusion.

Do not instead apply the closed-source theorem directly to S: S need
not be closed. Nor does ordinary completion automatically satisfy the
literal Definition 2.17(4c), which asks for (K,U)-completions on its
chosen test convention. The semantic bridge remains open.

## 3. Generic absolute two-support descent fails: a four-vertex check

The two-small-support criterion from #172 is valid as a CONDITIONAL
completion theorem. It cannot be inferred solely from free-amalgam
geometry and a generator on each exclusive side.

Use vertices a,r,s,b. There is a unary P predicate on a,b, a binary
closure relation R with exactly R(a,r) and R(b,s), and a symmetric
irreflexive ordinary edge relation E. Let E make BOTH triples
{a,r,s} and {b,r,s} complete, but omit the edge {a,b}. The only closure
rule has a singleton P-root and closure relation R.

Write L={a,r,s}, T={b,r,s}, Q={r,s}, and C=L amalgam_Q T.
Then C,L,T,Q are U-closed; L,T,Q are ordinarily irreducible. The set
{a,b} generates C, and the identity projection has no generator collision.
Nevertheless:

    USize(C) = USize(L) = USize(T) = USize(Q) = 2.

The singleton hulls in C are {a,r}, {b,s}, {r}, and {s}. Thus NO target
support of size at most one generates a hull containing the common
boundary Q. In fact no free decomposition of C can have both closed
sides of rank at most one: the edge {r,s} must be present in one side,
but no closed rank-at-most-one substructure contains it.

The dependency-free exhaustive script
`scripts/check_theorem218_boundary_budget.py` checks all subsets,
computes the ranks, checks the specified free decomposition and confirms
that none of the five supports of cardinality at most one covers Q.
It also checks the stronger no-two-rank-one-free-cover assertion.
The script was run locally with all assertions passing. This finite
regression is computationally checked, NOT itself a Lean theorem.

This is NOT a counterexample to Lemma 2.30 or Theorem 2.18. For example,
adding the one missing ordinary edge produces a U-closed irreducible
completion of C. The two pieces are already irreducible closed structures
and can be amalgamated over Q; their absolute ranks do not have to drop.
Nor does the example assert that every chosen Picture/Hales--Jewett
history contains this configuration. It rules out the proposed GENERIC
inference, so an actual history argument needs additional information.

## 4. What free-amalgam geometry really gives: relative generator drops

`Ramsey/ClosureRelativeGeneratorDrop.lean` proves the precise valid
replacement. Let G generate a finite free source E amalgam_R F. If a
member of G lies outside E, then there is a finite S_E in E with

    |S_E| < |G|,
    cl_U^E(S_E union R) = E.

The analogous statement holds for F. Thus, with generators on both
exclusive sides and |G|<=j+1, both sides need at most j ADDITIONAL
generators once the whole common root is supplied.

The proof takes exactly those generators landing on the given side.
Their cardinality is bounded by G with one excluded generator erased;
the previous free-amalgam generation theorem supplies generation after
adding the common root. No projection injectivity, rank-minimising
choice of G, or assumption that the sides have smaller total carriers
is used.

This is a relative generator bound, not an absolute U-size bound.
The four-vertex example shows the difference is essential: over Q the
left side needs only a and the right side only b, even though Q itself
has rank two.

## 5. Revised next construction obligation

Do not require every mixed cut to produce two absolute rank-j targets.
Retain the actual earlier-copy/history data and allow a piece already
known completable from an earlier stage to be used without a false
absolute rank-drop argument. For the remaining pieces, the relative
bound in Section 4 suggests the right boundary-aware invariant.

The unproved task is to establish that invariant from the REAL Picture
construction and the original class/local-finiteness hypotheses. In
particular, a completion over a boundary must preserve the SAME fixed
closed K-member so that the independently chosen targets can be glued.
The closed-boundary compatibility theorem from #171 remains available,
but a relative completion-existence hypothesis must not be introduced
as an oracle or silently inferred from an absolute rank bound.

The final extraction back to original weak n-vertex tests and the literal
Definition 2.17 hypotheses must be checked after that stronger invariant
is established. The current patch does not claim the full rank increment,
Ramsey pass, or Theorem 2.18.
